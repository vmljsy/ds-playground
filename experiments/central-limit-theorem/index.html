Central Limit Theorem

Explore how sample means converge to a normal distribution

Statement

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n$ be i.i.d. random variables with mean $\mu$ and finite standard deviation $\sigma$. Then as $n \to \infty$:

$$\frac{\bar{X}_n - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} N(0, 1)$$

In Plain English

No matter the shape of the population, the distribution of sample means will be approximately normal for large enough sample sizes, centered at μ with standard error σ/√n.

How It Works

  1. Choose a population: Select any distribution — skewed, flat, bimodal, or discrete
  2. Set sample size (n): This is how many observations are in each random sample
  3. Draw many samples: We draw thousands of samples and compute the mean of each one
  4. Watch the magic: The distribution of those sample means forms a bell curve — that's the CLT!

Key Insights

Sample size matters: Larger n → better normal approximation AND narrower spread (SE = σ/√n)
Symmetry helps: Symmetric populations (like Uniform) converge faster than highly skewed ones (like Exponential)
It works for discrete too: Even dice rolls produce normally-distributed means!
When CLT fails: The CLT requires finite variance. Heavy-tailed distributions like the Cauchy distribution do not satisfy this — their sample means never converge to normal.

Why It Matters

Application How CLT helps
Confidence Intervals Build intervals using the normal distribution
Hypothesis Testing z-tests and t-tests rely on normality of X̄
Quality Control Control charts assume sample means are normal
Polling & Surveys Margin of error calculations
